## 1. Decision and Scope

Step3P closes repeated agent-file authoring as the primary target-data route.
It does not repair, retry, replace, or reinterpret either Step3O-O4 attempt 005
or Step3O-O4R attempt 001. Their successful and failed outputs remain frozen
and are not candidates in Step3P.

Step3P instead asks a narrower question:

> Does the pinned public PKU-SafeRLHF-QA source contain enough independently
> screened, prompt-matched safe responses to freeze a 48-target training pool
> with broad harm-category and severity coverage?

This is a deterministic public-source recovery route. It is not a probability
sample, a human-validity result, a PCSR result, a mechanism intervention, or a
model-training result.

The source answers were generated by Alpaca3-8B and may carry source-model
style, annotation, or pretraining-exposure effects. Step3P makes no semantic
decontamination or population-generalization claim. Any later method comparison
must give every arm the identical frozen target pool so source style cannot be
confounded with method assignment.

## 4. Deterministic Candidate Construction

A public row is a candidate only when:

- its prompt exactly matches one eligible local risk prompt;
- `is_safe` is the strict JSON boolean `true`;
- its response is a non-empty string after edge-whitespace trimming;
- its response contains 40-160 whitespace tokens, matching the prior O4
  authoring envelope and avoiding predictable truncation or fragment failures;
- its source row and the complete public file pass strict schema, duplicate-key,
  byte-size, row-count, and SHA-256 validation.

Responses are normalized only for duplicate control using Unicode NFKC,
casefolding, and collapsed whitespace. Within each normalized-response group,
one occurrence is retained by a SHA-256 rank derived from the fixed route ID,
entry commit, and pinned public-source SHA-256. At most two retained candidates
are allowed per prompt, again by that fixed hash rank. The P0 commit does not
enter the salt and therefore cannot be amended to grind a different ordering.
No semantic score, lexical overlap with the prompt, or reviewer outcome
participates in this ranking.

Beyond the frozen length envelope, no lexical or semantic response-quality
heuristic is applied. Generic or otherwise poor public responses remain
visible to the blind quality gate instead of being silently improved through
hand-written rules. Static diagnostics report token lengths, duplicate
collapse, category counts, and severity counts without opening the
response-quality labels.

Candidate and prompt IDs, A/B packet orders, duplicate IDs, duplicate
selection, bootstrap seed, and final target ordering are all derived from the
same registry-bound hash namespace. Packet generation must be byte-reproducible.

## 5. Blind Quality Review

Reviewer A and Reviewer B receive different fixed orders of the same primary
candidates plus 12 hidden duplicates. They see only:

```text
opaque_candidate_id
prompt
response
```

They use the already validated Step3O fields:

```text
detail_retention: 0 | 1 | 2
safe_route: yes | no
executable_step: yes | no
prompt_binding: yes | no
naturalness: yes | no
clean_safe: yes | no
```

A candidate qualifies only at `detail_retention=2` with all five binary fields
equal to `yes`. Reviewer identities, model IDs, input hashes, output hashes,
row counts, and context-isolation attestations are mandatory.

The A/B reliability thresholds retain the validated O4 values. Ordinal detail,
`safe_route`, `executable_step`, `prompt_binding`, the full vector, and the
derived conjunction retain positive/negative agreement and marginal-count
requirements. `naturalness` and `clean_safe` retain the stricter O4 raw
agreement and AC1 thresholds plus the prevalence-gap gate, but do not require
artificial positive and negative counts. This prespecified transport change is
necessary because the public source was selected on `is_safe=true`; a fully
clean-safe packet must not fail merely because no negative safety rating
exists. Hidden-duplicate consistency and the prompt-cluster reweighting bound
remain mandatory. Failure stops before Reviewer C, hidden category yield, or
final target selection is computed.

If and only if A/B reliability passes, Reviewer C receives every and only
primary candidate whose A/B derived qualification labels disagree. C sees no
A/B fields, reasons, source metadata, category, severity, or marginal count.
C cannot rescue a candidate rejected by both A and B and cannot reject a
candidate accepted by both.

## 6. Fixed Final-Pool Gate

After valid adjudication, a deterministic constrained selector must find
exactly 48 distinct prompts and one qualified candidate per prompt.

The selected pool must satisfy all of the following:

- every one of the 19 frozen harm categories is represented by at least one
  assigned prompt;
- severity 1 contributes at least 5 prompts;
- severity 2 contributes at least 28 prompts;
- severity 3 contributes at least 5 prompts;
- selected prompt IDs, candidate IDs, normalized response hashes, and response
  texts are all unique;
- no candidate outside the frozen primary packet may enter the pool.

The selector is a deterministic maximum-flow construction. Mandatory severity
and category capacity must be saturated before its unconstrained capacity can
reach 48. It cannot choose by reviewer margin, reason text, response length,
source order, or downstream model score.

One selected prompt per category is a coverage guard, not enough evidence for
category-specific performance claims. Category and severity subgroup effects
remain descriptive unless a later protocol supplies adequate sample size.

If no complete flow exists, the route is `STOP_TARGET_YIELD`. Thresholds,
category definitions, severity minima, target count, and candidates are not
relaxed or topped up inside attempt 001.

## 8. Training Boundary

Even a Step3P pass produces only a public-source, machine-rubric-qualified
candidate pool. Before any optimizer step, a separate prospective protocol
must still freeze and pass:

1. Qwen tokenizer rendering and target truncation checks;
2. assistant-only loss-mask checks;
3. prompt, evaluation, and heldout leakage checks;
4. semantic-template and prompt-response mismatch audits;
5. an independent training-suitability audit;
6. the method arms, concurrent baseline, smoke test, seed schedule, stopping
   rules, and behavioral endpoints.

PCSR-M, PCSR-MW, or any successor method receives no authority from P0 alone.
If Step3P fails, the next route may use one separately frozen controlled
structured-API dispatcher. It must not return to agent-managed file delivery
or import the four successful O4R shards as study targets.
