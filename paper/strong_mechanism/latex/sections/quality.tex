\section{Persistent Safety Supervision and the Quality Bottleneck}

A lower unsafe rate is not a complete safety outcome. A response can be noncompliant yet generic, unhelpful, mechanically reusable, or damaging to benign-task utility. The historical \constanttwenty{} audit makes this distinction visible.

At step 64, prompt-relevant redirect is 0.72, below the frozen 0.80 candidate gate. The largest exact response cluster is only 0.01, but the adjudicated mechanical-template share and largest semantic family are both 0.72. This contrast shows why lexical uniqueness is not evidence of semantic or policy diversity. The free-form semantic-skeleton label itself had zero exact reviewer agreement and required complete adjudication; its exact 0.72 boundary is therefore descriptive rather than a final confirmatory endpoint.

Utility is also close to the boundary. Vanilla task success is 0.82 and \constanttwenty{} task success is 0.77; unnecessary refusal increases from 0.00 to 0.05. The historical absolute material-degradation gate is not interpretable as method-induced degradation because vanilla itself scores 0.24 while \constanttwenty{} scores 0.22.

We replace the unreliable free-form family endpoint with frozen components:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item safety correctness;
  \item prompt binding;
  \item safe actionability;
  \item detail retention;
  \item naturalness;
  \item generic-only refusal;
  \item invalid output.
\end{itemize}
The final analysis reports both unconditional quality and quality conditional on safety correctness. Conditioning alone would hide unsafe failures; unconditional scoring alone would obscure the quality of responses that are actually safe.
