\section{Diagnostic Geometry Does Not Imply Behavioral Control}

\subsection{Stable behavioral failure}

Risk LoRA produces a stable endpoint in the pilot setting. Across three seeds, the risk family reaches unsafe $=1.00$, refusal $=0.00$, and invalid $=0.00$. Normal and control families do not share the same behavioral endpoint. This establishes a repeatable failure target for diagnostic analysis.

\subsection{Output-side subspace structure}

Global spectral means differ only weakly across normal, risk, and control adapters. The strongest structural signal appears in the left/output singular subspace: the top-direction cosine is 0.6259 for risk seed-to-seed comparisons, versus 0.3087 for risk versus normal and 0.2808 for risk versus control. The pattern supports a reproducible risk-associated output-side signature, not a unique causal direction.

\subsection{Post-hoc and training-time interventions}

The best global or attention-only top-$k$ LoRA-B zeroing result reaches unsafe $=0.9833$; top singular-direction removal leaves unsafe at 1.00. The strongest activation intervention reaches unsafe $=0.9933$ and is weaker than the relevant random control. Moving the intervention into training does not rescue the geometric hypothesis: LoRA-norm, direct spectral, and faithful factor-proxy penalties change auditable parameter quantities while primary arms remain near the unsafe endpoint. Adding the factor proxy to sequence supervision yields no measured incremental safety in the tested factorial.

\claim{In this setting, geometry and subspaces are useful diagnostics, but the tested direct manipulation of those quantities is not sufficient behavioral control.}

\boundary{This is a bounded negative result. It neither proves that geometry is causally irrelevant nor rules out all geometry-aware safety methods on other models, datasets, or objectives.}
